AI Footprint Estimator

Compare the energy, carbon, and water cost of different AI operations — adjusted to your local electricity grid.
How to use: Move the sliders on the left to match how you actually use AI. Pick your region (and a specific model where it matters) so the math reflects your local electricity grid. The panel on the right shows the resulting annual footprint, and what it's roughly equivalent to in everyday terms. All numbers are order-of-magnitude estimates — useful for comparison, not for compliance reporting. See the methodology section below for what's included and what isn't.

    About the numbers. Estimates are mid-range public figures and should be read as order-of-magnitude — actual costs vary widely by hardware, datacenter PUE, model architecture, batch size, and grid mix.
    Inference per-query energy. Luccioni, Jernite & Strubell (2024), “Power Hungry Processing: ⚡ Watts ⚡ Driving the Cost of AI Deployment?”; de Vries (2023), “The growing energy footprint of artificial intelligence,” Joule. ~0.3 Wh per search, ~0.3–1 Wh per small-LLM reply, ~3 Wh per frontier reply, ~3 Wh per image. Video estimate (~50 Wh/sec) is a rough public extrapolation pending audited figures.
    AI agent tasks. An agent run is a chain of model calls plus tool invocations. We estimate ~10 calls per quick task (≈20 Wh) and ~100 calls per complex task (≈250 Wh), assuming a mix of small and frontier-class models. Per-task energy is highly task-dependent — published audits are scarce. See Stojkovic et al. (2024), “Towards Greener LLM Serving,” and Hugging Face's AI environmental impact primer for related context.
    Training. Patterson et al. (2021), “Carbon Emissions and Large Neural Network Training” & Patterson et al. (2022), “The Carbon Footprint of Machine Learning Training Will Plateau, Then Shrink.” GPT-3: ≈1,287 MWh, 552 t CO₂; BERT-base: ≈1.5 MWh. Llama 2 70B: ≈688 MWh (Meta Llama 2 paper). Frontier (GPT-4-class) ≈50,000 MWh per Epoch AI compute estimates.
    Water. Li, Yang, Islam & Ren (2023), “Making AI Less Thirsty: Uncovering and Addressing the Secret Water Footprint of AI Models.” ~0.5 L per 20–50 GPT-3-class queries; ~700,000 L for GPT-3 training (Microsoft US data centers). Operational water scales with energy use and regional Water Usage Effectiveness.
    Grid carbon intensity (gCO₂/kWh). Countries: Ember Yearly Electricity Data 2024 and IEA Electricity Information 2024 (2023 generation averages). US states: EPA eGRID 2022. Canadian provinces: Canada Energy Regulator and National Inventory Report 2023. Australian states: DCCEEW National Greenhouse Accounts 2023. China regional grids: Ministry of Ecology & Environment baseline emission factors.
    Compute (data center) location. The artifact cannot directly observe which region served a given query — major consumer AI products don't expose that to end users. The presets are likely served from estimates based on public statements about provider infrastructure (AWS Bedrock/Anthropic, Azure OpenAI region availability, GCP Vertex AI regions). Region-level grid intensity for cloud regions is approximated from the local state/country average. For exact figures, see cloud-provider carbon dashboards (AWS, GCP, Azure) — accessible only to the account holder.
    Equivalencies. EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator (gas passenger vehicle ≈ 400 g CO₂/mi; EV ≈ 85 g CO₂/mi on US grid; mature tree ≈ 21 kg CO₂/yr; average US home ≈ 10,800 kWh/yr); 9 W LED bulb; 5 Wh per smartphone charge; 77 Wh per hour of Netflix streaming (Carbon Trust 2021); Bitcoin transaction ≈ 830 kWh (Cambridge CBECI).

    What this tool does and doesn't include · Operational only · Operational + embodied

    Included in the estimates:

    • Operational energy for the AI compute itself (CPU/GPU/TPU)
    • Datacenter cooling and overhead (typical hyperscale PUE ≈ 1.12)
    • Carbon based on your selected grid mix (country, sub-region, or specific cloud region)
    • Direct cooling water at the datacenter
    • Embodied energy, carbon, and water — manufacturing GPUs and servers, building the datacenter, end-of-line semiconductor fab water. Added as mid-range multipliers on top of operational: +12% energy, +15% carbon, +25% water.

    Not included (would change the picture in ways this tool doesn't model):

    • Embodied carbon, energy, and water of hardware — manufacturing GPUs, servers, and datacenter buildings. Toggle Scope → + Embodied at the top of the results panel to add these as mid-range estimates.
    • Network transit and your device — the energy from your ISP through transit providers to the datacenter, plus your laptop/phone running while you wait. Small per query, but non-zero.
    • Training-cost amortization — for popular models like ChatGPT, training divided across billions of queries is tiny; for niche or rarely-used models, training can dominate the lifetime cost.
    • Provider-reported sustainability claims — many cloud providers buy renewable-energy credits or offsets. This tool uses physical grid intensity, not "as-reported net-zero" figures.
    • End-of-life e-waste — retiring GPUs every 3–5 years creates significant waste streams beyond what the embodied figures capture.
    • Specific query characteristics — output length, context size, and reasoning depth can swing per-query energy by 10–100×. The figures here are mid-range averages.

    Brief methodology

    Operational only: Operational + embodied: Energy figures come from peer-reviewed research (Luccioni et al., Patterson et al., Strubell et al., de Vries), published provider model cards (Meta Llama, Mistral, Anthropic, Google), and Hugging Face's AI Energy Score benchmarks. For each operation, a single mid-range per-instance value is used; the optional model picker on chat and image rows lets you swap in a specific model's published or estimated figure.

    Carbon = energy × grid carbon intensity (gCO₂/kWh). Grid intensities are 2022–2023 generation averages: countries from Ember Yearly Electricity Data 2024 and IEA Electricity 2024; US states from EPA eGRID 2022; Canadian provinces from Canada Energy Regulator + National Inventory Report 2023; Australian states from DCCEEW; China regional grids from MEE baseline factors. The "Compute location" presets approximate where major US providers run inference based on public statements about their infrastructure.

    Water is operational + electricity-related, modeled as a flat per-instance value. Site- and season-specific water variation is not modeled.

    Embodied (when scope = "+ Embodied"): applied as flat percentage multipliers on top of operational figures — +12% energy (Patterson et al. 2022; Microsoft sustainability reports), +15% carbon (Gupta et al. "Chasing Carbon" 2022; Google / Microsoft public methodologies), +25% water (TSMC and Intel semiconductor-fab disclosures for advanced-node manufacturing). These are mid-range; actual embodied share grows in absolute terms as operational decarbonizes, so for very clean grids (Vermont, Quebec, Norway) embodied can dominate. Tool uses fixed percentages for simplicity.

    All numbers should be read as order of magnitude. They're useful for comparison ("agent vs chat reply," "Virginia vs Oregon," "10/day vs 100/day") but not for compliance reporting.

    Data current as of November 2024. Grid intensities update annually as new EPA / Ember / CER datasets release. Inference figures will evolve as providers publish more transparent reporting.
